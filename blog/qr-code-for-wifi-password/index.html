How to Make a QR Code for a Wi-Fi Password

September 10, 2026

Reading out a 20-character Wi-Fi password letter by letter, or watching a guest mistype it three times, is a small but very avoidable annoyance. Most phones have supported scanning a QR code straight into their Wi-Fi settings since around 2019 — no app required — and you can generate one for free, right in your browser, in under a minute.

The trick: a text format phones already understand

A Wi-Fi QR code isn't a special kind of QR code — it's a regular QR code that encodes a specific line of text, which phone camera apps recognize and offer to join automatically. The format looks like this:

WIFI:T:WPA;S:YourNetworkName;P:YourPassword;;

Each part means something specific:

  • T is the security type: WPA for WPA, WPA2 or WPA3 (almost every home router made in the last decade), WEP for very old routers, or nopass for an open network with no password.
  • S is your network name (SSID), typed exactly as it appears, including capital letters.
  • P is the password. For a nopass network, leave this part out entirely along with the P: — don't leave it blank.
  • If your network name or password itself contains a semicolon, comma, colon or backslash, put a backslash right before each one (for example My\;Network) so phones don't misread the boundaries between fields.

Steps (free, no signup, no app)

  1. Build your own line using the format above — swap in your real network name after S: and your real password after P:, keeping the semicolons exactly where they are.
  2. Open the QR Code Generator, paste that line into the text box, pick a size, and click Generate.
  3. Download the PNG. Print it and stick it near your router or entryway, or just show the image on a screen — any modern phone camera pointed at it will recognize it as a Wi-Fi network and offer a one-tap Join button.
  4. Test it yourself with your own phone before you print it or hand it to a guest, so you catch any typo in the network name or password first.

A few things worth knowing

  • This works with the plain camera app on iOS 11+ and Android 10+ — no extra scanner app needed. Older phones may need a dedicated QR scanner app instead, which will usually still read it correctly.
  • For a hidden network (one that doesn't broadcast its name), add H:true; right before the final double semicolon, e.g. WIFI:T:WPA;S:YourNetworkName;P:YourPassword;H:true;;
  • The QR code encodes your password as plain text, so it's exactly as sensitive as the password itself — fine to stick on your fridge for house guests, not something to post somewhere the general public can photograph if you'd rather they not have your Wi-Fi.
